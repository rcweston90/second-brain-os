# topics — the wiki

**Purpose:** the vault's wiki — one hub page per subject you keep returning to. A topic page is a map in your own words: your current position on the subject, links to where the evidence lives, and the questions you're still carrying. It's reference you maintain with no finish line, which is why it's an area.

**A subject earns a page when it recurs** — shows up in two or more projects, essays, or clipping runs. Before that, the material just lives where it is. A wiki page for something touched once is designing, not shipping.

**A topic page holds:**
- Your current position, in your own words — a few paragraphs, not an essay
- Links out to the evidence: the projects that touch it, the library notes that shaped it, the essays it produced
- Open questions worth carrying forward

**What it is not:**
- Not project notes — those stay in `1-projects/<project>/`; the project links *into* the topic
- Not other people's writing — that's `3-library/`, linked from here
- Not a flat reference list — those live in `lists/`

**Linking is the point.** Projects link into their topics; topics link out to evidence. When a project archives, its topic page is what survives — the residue of what the work taught you.

**Lifecycle question this folder answers:** *Do I keep coming back to this subject?*

*Example pages a working vault might hold: a craft topic, a tools-and-methods topic, a house style guide, a set of standing instructions for any agent. The pattern matters; the specific subjects are yours.*
